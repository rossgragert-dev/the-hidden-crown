# Story Bible: The Hidden Crown

Source of truth for story and systems design. (Originally authored as a claude.ai doc; this is the portable copy.)

## Premise & Pillars

**Logline:** A castle squire trains to become a knight while the player knows what he does not: he is the lost heir to the throne, and the kingdom's usurper is hunting him. The player's choices decide whether he becomes a benevolent sovereign or a monstrous one.

**Tone:** High fantasy. Bright, mythic and heroic, with a shadow real enough that giving in to it is tempting.

**The hook (dramatic irony):** A short prologue shows the player the night the true king fell and the infant prince was smuggled away. The squire never sees it. Every act plays on the gap between what the player knows and what Corin knows: the odd looks, the guarded mentor, the relic that glows at his touch.

**Design pillars**
- **Earned crown:** Corin becomes a knight through trials before he becomes a king. Power comes from growth, not birthright alone.
- **Choices with weight:** Every mercy and cruelty moves one visible compass, and the world answers.
- **Light or dark, same throne:** The player can end as a beloved sovereign or a feared tyrant. Both paths are complete, powerful and playable.
- **Four ways to fight:** Swords, staves, bows and arrows are all viable builds, each with a place in the story.

## The Realm of Auremark

A river-and-highland kingdom held together for 400 years by the **Oathcrown**, a relic that binds the king's bloodline to the land's magic. When a true king rules, harvests hold, border wards stay lit and magic flows cleanly. Without one, it frays.

**History**
1. **The Founding:** Aldaris the Oathbound made a pact with the Dawnroot, an ancient world-tree, and forged the crown from its heartwood and gold.
2. **The Long Peace:** Twelve generations of Aldaris kings. The Order of the Silver Thorn (knights) and the Collegium of Quill & Flame (mages) grew up around the throne.
3. **The Night of Ash:** Sixteen years ago Regent Maldric Vane poisoned King Roderan and burned the royal nursery. He claimed the infant prince died. He rules in the king's name, but the Oathcrown rejects him.
4. **The Fraying (now):** Blighted fields in the north, failing border wards, rising bandit and monster activity. Vane blames raiders and taxes harder.

**Regions:** Castle Hearthmoor (central highlands, the squire's home), The Greenmarch (farmland, first to feel the blight), Ironvale (mining holds, Hammerguilds), Veilfen (mist-bound marshes, old wild magic), Aurelith (royal capital, seat of the Oathcrown).

**Magic:** Drawn from the land through a staff or focus. **Dawn** heals, wards, sustains. **Dusk** drains, frightens, binds, commands. Both are powerful. The Oathcrown amplifies whichever the king leans toward, tying the morality compass to magic.

## Protagonist: Corin Ashdown

16-year-old squire at Castle Hearthmoor, raised by Ser Halvard Ashdown (he believes guardian and distant kinsman). Quick, stubborn, desperate to belong among knights without a noble name.

- **Strengths:** Natural leadership, talent with every weapon, an odd affinity for magic.
- **Flaws:** Impulsive, hungry for recognition, quietly afraid he is nobody. The compass plays on these: pride curdles into tyranny, the need to be loved can become real compassion.
- **Secret:** He is Prince Corwen Aldaris. His blood wakes the Oathcrown, but the Crown only answers a king who has proven himself. The player knows from the prologue. Corin learns in Act II.
- **Arc:** Squire (powerless, underestimated) to knight (earned) to king (the real test, since power exposes who he is).

## The Usurper: Regent Maldric Vane

Brilliant, disciplined administrator who believes the old line grew weak and Auremark needs a strong hand. He killed the king not out of greed but because he thought the kingdom would collapse under a dying monarch and a child heir.
- **Why he works:** He is competent. Taxes fund real roads and a real army. His tragedy: the Oathcrown refuses him, so the blight is his failure, and he cannot admit it.
- **Mirror role:** A preview of the tyrant path. If the player drifts toward cruelty Corin starts to sound like him. Late game can reveal Vane once swore the ideals Corin holds now.
- **Lieutenants:** Captain Sorrel Dunmoor (loyal blade), Veska (mistress of knives, Ashen Hand), Lord Garran Vale (Brannoch's father, Regency hammer).

## Morality Compass: The Crown's Weight

One slider, -100 (Tyrant) to +100 (Benevolent), starting at 0. Every meaningful choice moves it. Shown as a crown icon whose glow shifts gold to ember-red.

| Range | Title | What changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| +70 to +100 | The Beloved | Dawn magic empowered. Villages rally, factions join freely, companions loyal. |
| +30 to +69 | The Just | Strong faction support. Dawn unlocked, Dusk costs more. |
| -29 to +29 | The Wanderer | Neutral and flexible. |
| -69 to -30 | The Iron Hand | Fear works. Dusk unlocked, tax/conscription options, some allies leave. |
| -100 to -70 | The Monster King | Dusk empowered. Towns surrender, companions desert or turn, Oathcrown darkens. |

**What moves it:** mercy vs cruelty; generosity vs greed; honor vs expedience; magic source (Dusk casts nudge down, Dawn nudges up).

**Design rules:** both ends must be powerful (tyranny is not a punishment path); no choice is secretly correct (cruel solves fast, kind solves durably); companions comment live and some quests only appear at one end; Act III turns the slider into mechanics (court judgments, taxes, armies, Oathcrown power).

| Ending | How |
| --- | --- |

| The Steady Crown | Just or Wanderer tier. Stable realm, cracks left. |
| The Iron Throne | Iron Hand tier. Peace through force. |
| The Dusk Reign | Monster King tier. Dawnroot consumed, replaced by Corin's will. |

**Themes:** Power reveals character rather than creating it. A crown is earned by service, not blood. Mercy and cruelty both cost; the question is who pays. Whoever holds the throne decides what the kingdom becomes.

## Branching & Replayability

- **Compass path:** four tiers unlock exclusive side quests, dialogue, ability flavors and a different final boss form.
- **Faction alliances:** only two of four factions (Order, Collegium, Hammerguilds, Marshkin) join at Aurelith; the others become closed paths with their own missions.
- **Mutually exclusive missions:** in Act II one of two routes to the Spire.
- **Companion fates:** Halvard, Lysa, Brannoch, Sable, Ione can survive, desert, betray or die by flags.
- **Three builds:** Valor, Arcana, Wild play the same missions differently.
- **Loot:** rarer gear in later and replayed missions.
- **New Game+ (Crowned Mode):** keep best gear and skill points, harder enemies, sixth ending.

## Progression, Loot & Skill Trees (implemented)

Level cap 30; each level grants one skill point, more HP and mana.

| Rarity | Color | Extra stats | Drop weight |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Common | Grey | 0 | 60 |
| Uncommon | Green | 1 | 26 |
| Rare | Blue | 2 | 10 |
| Epic | Purple | 3 | 3.4 |
| Legendary | Orange | 3 plus bonus, named | 0.6 |

Five gear slots: Sword, Staff, Bow, Armor, Trinket, plus a cosmetic Outfit slot. Bosses always drop gear; chests roll better rarity; missions grant one guaranteed item.

| Tree | Style | Ability | Capstone |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Valor | Sword and shield | Shield Bash, then Whirlwind | Oath of the Knight: +15% damage, +20 Defense, knockback |
| Arcana | Staff, Dawn or Dusk | Nova (heals on Dawn, terrifies on Dusk) | Crownfire: +25% staff damage, pierce |
| Wild | Bow, crits, evasion | Volley (five arrows) | Deadeye: 2.6x crits |

Four tiers per tree; tier N unlocks after 3*(N-1) points in that tree. Respec costs gold. Dusk casts push the compass to Tyrant, Dawn to Benevolent.
